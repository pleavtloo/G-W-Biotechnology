AI narrows the search. The bench decides.

Four layers — data, models, tools and lab capacity — wired into one loop. Owning the loop is the difference between selling reagents and building a platform.

AI-guided design Wet-lab validation in-house 42 tools in catalogue Network capacity
01 Data

Assay and process data

Every project produces two kinds of output: the answer you asked for, and everything the bench learned on the way. Most suppliers keep the first and discard the second.

  • Lot-level records for every catalogue SKU
  • Protocol outcomes, including the conditions that failed
  • QC release data structured for re-use, not just filing
What it changes: the next project starts from a measured prior, not a guess.
02 Models

Design and optimisation models

Models do one job here: shrink the space of things worth testing. They propose sequences, formulations and protocol windows. They do not sign off on a result.

  • Protein and antibody candidate generation
  • Formulation and buffer screening
  • Protocol parameter optimisation against your assay
What it changes: fewer bench cycles between a spec and a working reagent.
03 Tools

Physical reagents

The layer your experiment actually touches. Designed against an assay rather than a catalogue slot, then released under a quality system that survives audit.

  • 42 catalogue tools across four families
  • GMP and RUO grades of the same design
  • Custom builds when the catalogue does not fit
What it changes: a reagent you can qualify once and scale without a redesign.
04 Lab network

Capacity to run the work

Our own laboratory handles design and validation. When volume exceeds one bench, qualified partner facilities absorb the overflow under our methods and our quality system.

  • 3,200 m² in-house R&D and GMP base
  • Qualified partner facilities for overflow
  • Automation roadmap published, not implied
What it changes: throughput stops being a function of one building.
04The loop

Design, test, learn, repeat.

Four steps, run for every project — including the ones that fail.

01
Define

Target, assay, constraints and the failure modes you already know about.

02
Design

Model-guided candidate generation, reviewed by an application scientist.

03
Validate

Bench testing in our lab — binding, potency, stability, lot consistency.

04
Scale

Transfer to GMP or partner capacity, with documentation that travels.

The loop is the product. A reagent shipped without its assay data is a commodity; the same reagent shipped with the data that shaped it is a head start.

05Boundaries

What we are not.

AI claims are cheap. Being specific about the boundary is what makes the rest credible.

We are not a model company.

We do not sell software licences or a foundation model. The models are internal tooling that makes our reagents and protocols better.

We are not a CRO.

We do not take on every assay. We take work that extends the tool stack — if it teaches the platform nothing, we will say so.

We are not automated yet.

High-throughput is a 2027 target, not a current capability. The roadmap is on the services page so you can plan against it.

See the loop run on your target.

Send an assay that is not behaving. We will tell you which layer is failing — and whether it is worth fixing with a tool, a protocol, or a different approach entirely.
